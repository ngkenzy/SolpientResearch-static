## 1. Investment thesis

**What it does:** Harmony is a commercial-stage biopharmaceutical company focused on sleep disorders. Its main product is WAKIX (pitolisant), a treatment for narcolepsy, with other products and programs in development. *Reported in company filings; product detail is general knowledge and not checked against the current 10-K.*

**How it makes money:** Prescription sales to patients through specialty pharmacies, with reimbursement from insurers. Net product revenue was $868M in FY2025 *(reported)*.

**Why customers choose it:** Physicians prescribe WAKIX for narcolepsy patients who need a treatment that is not a stimulant, and insurance coverage and patient support matter for uptake. *General knowledge.*

**Competitive advantages:** Patent protection on WAKIX, an established sales force, and a prescriber base. The advantage is temporary: it lasts only as long as patents or exclusivity hold. *Assessment.*

**Growth drivers:** WAKIX growth in narcolepsy, expanded indications and new products in development. Q2 2026 WAKIX net product revenue was about $261M, up 30% year over year *(reported in Q2 2026 preannouncement coverage)*.

**Major threats:** Loss of exclusivity (generic or competing product entry), competing treatments in development, reimbursement changes, and dependence on one or two products. *Assessment.* The exact patent expiry dates and settlement terms were not verified in this pass; they are the central unknown.

**AI opportunities and disruption:** Little direct AI exposure. AI could speed drug discovery at the margin, but it does not change the main risk here, which is patent-protected revenue. *Assessment.*

**What must be true:**
1. WAKIX keeps growing at double digits through at least 2028, with 2026 net product revenue at or above the $1.0–1.04B guidance.
2. Patent protection or competing-product timing lets the franchise run for the next 5–10 years.
3. Pipeline or new indications offset any erosion after exclusivity ends.
4. Management stability holds after the CFO's July 2026 departure.

**Bull thesis:** Revenue grew 21% in 2025 and is guided up 15–20% in 2026. Operating margin is about 24% on a GAAP basis and about 34% in the feed. The company generates about $300M a year of SBC-adjusted free cash flow and holds about $0.5B of net cash. The stock trades at a low multiple of cash flow.

**Bear thesis:** A single product drives most of the value. If generic or competing entry comes earlier than the market expects, revenue could fall sharply and the stock's cash-flow multiple would not protect the price. Growth is already slowing from 43% (2022) to 21% (2025).

## 9. Buffett and Lynch lenses

**Buffett-style: partial pass with a limit.** Understandability is moderate: the business is easy to describe, but its future depends on patent law and clinical data. The moat is narrow and time-limited, which Buffett would treat as a problem for a long-term holding. Returns on equity are good, cash generation is strong, and the balance sheet is net cash. Capital allocation has been conservative. The price is below the base value, but the value depends on an assumption about patent life that I cannot yet support. **Does it meet Buffett's principles?** Partly. It meets the business-quality and balance-sheet tests. It fails the durable-moat test until patent timing is known.

**Lynch-style: Fast Grower, with binary risk.** Revenue is growing 20% a year, which is fast for Lynch's categories. The story is simple: one drug, growing fast. Lynch would still ask what happens when the growth ends. PEG is not meaningful here: the 5-year EPS CAGR is inflated by the depressed FY21 base, and the growth rate is falling.

## 10. Capital allocation test

**Why own Harmony instead of an index?** Because the cash flow and the price make a strong return possible if WAKIX keeps its exclusivity for several years, which would justify the 22% three-year base case. If it does not, the stock is likely to fall well below today's price.

**The evidence does not yet support a compelling case that Harmony beats an index with high confidence.** The upside is large, but the downside depends on patent timing that is not verified. That makes the valuation uncertainty too high to classify with confidence.

**Classification: Not classified.** The valuation uncertainty is too high to say the stock is attractive at any price until exclusivity dates and litigation outcomes are verified. Once they are, re-run the model with a loss-of-exclusivity scenario, and reassess.

*Comparison with Jazz:* Harmony is cheaper, grows faster, and carries less debt, but has far more concentration. Jazz is the more diversified business; Harmony is the simpler bet.

## Final conclusion

**Is this a great business?** It is a strong business today: fast growth, a 24% GAAP operating margin, growing cash, and no debt that matters. It is not a durable great business until its patent protection and pipeline are known.

**Is the valuation attractive?** On cash flow, the stock looks cheap, at about 7x SBC-adjusted free cash flow and about 14x trailing earnings. That is only attractive if the franchise lasts. Without verified exclusivity dates, I cannot say the price is attractive.

**What return appears realistically achievable?** If exclusivity holds for five years, the base case is about 15% a year. If it does not, returns could be negative, and my model cannot yet quantify how negative.

**What could permanently impair the investment?** Early generic or competing-drug entry that removes most of WAKIX revenue faster than the pipeline can replace it.

**Does it justify allocating capital instead of owning the market?** Not yet. The upside is real, but the key uncertainty is one that could permanently reduce the value, and the evidence does not let me size it. Revisit once patent dates and litigation outcomes are verified. This is research, not personalized advice.
