## 1. Investment thesis

**What it does:** Genpact provides business process management and digital transformation services, mostly to large enterprises (finance and accounting, procurement, insurance and customer operations), plus a growing advanced technology solutions practice (AI, data, and software). *General knowledge; not checked against the current 10-K.*

**How it makes money:** Mostly multi-year contracts billed on time, transactions, or fixed fees. Revenue is recurring in the sense that large clients renew, but contracts are re-bid. *General knowledge.*

**Why customers choose it:** Process knowledge, scale, and the ability to run and improve operations, not just advise on them. *General knowledge.*

**Competitive advantages:** Deep process expertise in finance and insurance, and long client relationships. Advantages are moderate; the industry is competitive and pricing is pressured. *Assessment.*

**Growth drivers:** Advanced technology solutions (reported to be up 24% year over year in Q2 2026); AI-enabled services. *Reported in press coverage of the Q2 2026 release; verify in the 8-K.*

**Major threats:** Generative AI reduces the labor hours that outsourcing contracts are priced on. Clients may bring work in-house or push for lower unit prices. *Assessment.*

**AI opportunity and disruption:** AI can raise margins on existing contracts and create new consulting and data work. The same tools can cut the effort needed per transaction, which would shrink billable volume. The net effect is the central question. *Assessment.*

**What must be true:**
1. Revenue keeps growing about 7% a year (2026 guidance is at least $5.435B, up about 7%).
2. Operating margins hold at about 15% as AI tools are adopted.
3. Clients accept AI-driven pricing without large unit-price cuts.
4. Free cash flow stays strong enough to fund buybacks and the dividend.

**Bull thesis:** Genpact is growing revenue about 7%, earns about a 15% operating margin, and trades at about 9x company-guided non-GAAP EPS. It has already grown EPS from $1.91 (2021) to $3.13 (2025) on GAAP. AI could expand its margins and service mix.

**Bear thesis:** Services revenue is exposed to AI-driven productivity. Growth is only moderate, leverage is higher than peers on a lease-inclusive basis, and a low multiple may reflect expected decline rather than mispricing.

## 9. Buffett and Lynch lenses

**Buffett-style: partial pass.** Understandability is moderate: services businesses are harder to model than consumer brands. The moat is narrow. ROE is about 22% and free cash flow is solid, with a balance sheet that is adequate rather than strong. Capital allocation has been reasonable: share count has fallen. The price is well below the base value, but the base value depends on margins holding in a changing industry.

**Lynch-style: Stalwart.** EPS grew about 13% a year over five years, with revenue growth in the mid single digits. Lynch would call it a Stalwart rather than a Fast Grower. PEG is not meaningful here, because EPS growth is volatile and the 2026 EPS growth rate is not established. The story is not simple because AI could change the business model.

## 10. Capital allocation test

**Why own Genpact instead of an index?** Only if you believe AI supports, rather than erodes, its services pricing, and that its margin holds near 15%. Then the base case returns about 8–10% a year from today's price, with a dividend, and the stock trades near 9x guided earnings. If AI cuts billable volume, the bear case returns about −4% a year over five years. Downside is limited by the low multiple but not eliminated.

**The evidence does not yet support a compelling case that Genpact beats an index with high confidence.** The Q3 2026 print on Nov. 5 is a near-term checkpoint.

**Classification: Attractive below $51.** At $37 the stock is below the 35% margin-of-safety price on the base case. Because the base value depends on margins, I would not call it attractive at the current price without the qualifier.

*Comparison with peers:* against Cognizant, Genpact has higher growth and a similar multiple; against EXL, it is slower-growing and much cheaper. Against Accenture, the valuation discount is larger than the difference in quality.

## Final conclusion

**Is this a great business?** Solid but not great. It has steady growth, a consistent 15% operating margin, and a falling share count. Its moat is narrow and its industry is exposed to AI-driven productivity gains.

**Is the valuation attractive?** Yes, relative to its history and peers, at about 9x guided earnings and 12x trailing GAAP. The base-case value is well above the price, but my own defensible range is lower, so the margin of safety is smaller than the model suggests.

**What return appears realistically achievable?** About 8–10% a year over five years in the base case, with about 2% from the dividend. The bear case is about −4% a year over five years.

**What could permanently impair it?** Clients reducing billable work faster than Genpact can replace it with higher-margin AI services, combined with price cuts on existing contracts.

**Does it justify allocating capital instead of owning the market?** Only modestly. The base case is about the same as a broad index, with more risk. It suits an investor who is comfortable with services-sector AI risk and who will wait for a price near $51 or below. This is research, not personalized advice.
